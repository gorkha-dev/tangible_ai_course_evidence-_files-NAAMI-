Here is the exact, low-stress lesson plan for Day 1.

Day 1 Lesson Plan: Crack the Hangeul Code
1. The Mindset Shift (5 Minutes)
The Hook: Tell them to forget everything they know about reading Chinese characters or memorizing English spelling. Hangeul was engineered in 1443 by a king who wanted everyone to read. It's a phonemic alphabet—it literally diagrams what your mouth and tongue are doing.

2. Introduce the "Building Block" Secret (15 Minutes)
Explain that Korean blocks sounds into syllables. Every syllable block is like a mini-box containing Consonant + Vowel (and sometimes a final consonant called a batchim, which we ignore for today).

Show them the 4 basic vowels that look like lines and dashes:

I (ee sound, like in meet)

ㅏ (ah sound, like in father)

ㅜ (oo sound, like in boot)

ㅗ (oh sound, like in boat)

3. Match with 4 Easy Consonants (15 Minutes)
Teach 4 consonants that have direct English equivalents so they get an immediate win:

ㅁ = M

ㄴ = N

ㅅ = S

ㅇ = Silent placeholder when leading a syllable (or ng at the end).

4. The "Aha!" Moment: Combining Them (15 Minutes)
Show them how to snap the pieces together into actual words:

ㅇ+ㅏ=아 (A, like the "ah" sound when the doctor checks your throat).

ㄴ+ㅏ=나 (Na, meaning I / me).

ㅁ+ㅏ+ㄴ+ㅏ=만나 (Manna, part of the verb to meet—though keep it simple with just Na for today).

ㅅ+ㅏ+ㄹ+ㅏ+ㅁ=사람 (Saram, meaning person).

5. Three Survival Slang/Polite Words to Walk Away With (10 Minutes)
Don't force grammar yet. Give them three high-frequency words they can use immediately:

안녕하세요 (Annyeonghaseyo): Hello.

감사합니다 (Gamsahamnida): Thank you.

네 (Ne): Yes.
